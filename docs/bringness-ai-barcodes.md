# Shared barcode catalog

Bringness AI stores shared product names, normalized GTINs, stock units and contents per pack in its separate AI database. Business stock, purchase prices, account identities and receipts are never returned by the shared catalog endpoint. There is no METRO catalog connection or external product lookup in this release.

Restaurants, suppliers and platform administrators can submit individual records or UTF-8 CSVs (maximum 200 products / 200 KB). CSV supports semicolon, comma and tab separators with automatic detection. Users map barcode, product name, stock unit and contents per pack to distinct columns; optional language-code columns and additional supplier columns are supported. Common German, English, Turkish and other international headers and unit names are suggested automatically. A validated preview displays the first 10 rows before a separate import action; preview writes nothing. Normalized units are `piece`, `kg`, `l`. Unicode product names stay in their original language; language metadata is stored, with no automatic translation or complete interface localization. Mixed quantities such as `32 x 100 g` and unsupported weight conversions require correction before importing. Quoted fields, escaped quotes, BOM, decimal commas in semicolon-separated CSV and multiline fields are supported. The full file validates before an atomic import. CSV imports create catalog suggestions, not inventory or marketplace offers.

GTIN-8/12/13/14 check digits are validated; zero padding to 14 digits preserves leading zeros and unifies equivalent GTIN representations. Internal variable-weight or non-GTIN codes need a future explicit adapter. Pack counts for pieces must be integers. A 32-piece pack received as three packs adds 96 pieces.

All submissions initially remain pending. Platform administrators can approve or reject entries, including their own CSV imports. At most one approved entry exists for a GTIN. Approving a conflicting entry returns the previous approved record to pending; it does not rewrite history. Changes are audited. Suppliers are not automatically treated as verified. Pending suggestions are visible to other authenticated businesses and explicitly require checking against the physical pack.

Restaurant receipts require explicit confirmation and whole pack counts. The first receipt creates a stock item or links an existing owned ingredient of the same unit and location. Later receipts reuse the saved GTIN-to-stock mapping. Account locking and request-key fingerprints prevent retry duplication and reject reuse of a key with changed data. Receipts preserve the selected immutable catalog-entry reference and actual stock delta. Once booked, correct mistakes through the existing stock correction flow.

The workspace has a Strichcode-Katalog tab for all roles. Suppliers can copy shared pack data into a new marketplace product form, then supply their own price and minimum order quantity. Camera scanning uses native BarcodeDetector where supported, stops tracks on navigation/page exit and falls back to keyboard/handscanner entry on unsupported browsers. Actual camera behavior still needs device testing. No external barcode CDN or image upload is used.

Validation: embedded PostgreSQL API tests cover shared access, canonical lookups, invalid checksums, fractional piece rejection, CSV atomic rollback, moderation authorization and replacement, tenant isolation, stock-unit mismatches, 96-piece receipt, retry fingerprint conflicts and persisted mappings. DOM tests cover the catalog tab, lookup, stored pack size, calculated total and receipt UI. Existing account/order/recipe/POS isolation workflows remain covered.
